# Computational Psychiatry & AI/LLM for Mental Health Research

> Research compiled: March 2026
> Purpose: Supporting Limbic-Flow project development

---

## 1. Overview

Computational psychiatry applies mathematical and computational models to understand mental health disorders, predict treatment outcomes, and develop AI-powered diagnostic tools. Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have opened new frontiers for mental health applications.

---

## 2. Key Research Areas & Recent Papers

### 2.1 LLM-Based Psychiatric Assessment

**BioLLMAgent: Hybrid Framework for Computational Psychiatry** (March 2026)
- Authors: Zuo Fei, Kezhi Wang, Xiaomin Chen, Yizhou Huang
- Addresses the trade-off between interpretability (traditional RL models) and behavioral realism (LLM agents)
- Introduces a hybrid framework combining structural interpretability with realistic behavior generation
- [arXiv:2603.0xxxx](https://arxiv.org)

**MoodAngels: Retrieval-augmented Multi-agent Framework for Psychiatry Diagnosis** (June 2025)
- Authors: Mengxi Xiao, Ben Liu, He Li, et al.
- Uses retrieval-augmented generation for psychiatric diagnosis
- Maintains strong utility for machine learning applications in mental health assessment
- Bridges important gaps in AI-assisted mental health assessment

**AI Models for Depressive Disorder Detection and Diagnosis: A Review** (August 2025)
- Authors: Dorsa Macky Aleagha, Payam Zohari, Mostafa Haghir Chehreghani
- Proposes a hierarchical taxonomy structured by:
  - Primary clinical task (diagnosis vs. prediction)
  - Data modality (text, speech, neuroimaging, multimodal)
  - Computational model class (GNNs, LLMs, hybrid approaches)
- Key finding: Graph neural networks predominate in current approaches

### 2.2 Computational Models of Psychiatric Disorders

**Depression as a Disorder of Distributional Coding** (July 2025)
- Authors: Matthew Botvinick, Zeb Kurth-Nelson, Timothy Muller, Will Dabney
- Integrates pathophysiology research (dopamine in depression) with computational approaches
- Provides a new theoretical framework for understanding depression

**The Role of Affect and Priors in the Generation of Hallucinations in Early Psychosis** (December 2025)
- Authors: Timothy Friesen, Philomène Labilloy, Deven Parekh, et al.
- Focuses on schizophrenia and psychotic symptoms
- Aims to develop biomarkers of early psychosis
- Key concepts: Affect, Priors, Computational Psychiatry, Clinical High Risk Population

**Perceptual Multistability: A Window for Understanding Psychiatric Disorders** (June 2025)
- Authors: Shervin Safavi, Danaé Rolland, Philipp Sterzer, et al.
- Computational approaches to understanding perceptual differences in clinical populations
- Differences in temporal dynamics and information integration during percept formation

### 2.3 Neuroimaging & Brain Analysis

**Brain-HGCN: Hyperbolic Graph Convolutional Network for Brain Functional Network Analysis** (September 2025)
- Authors: Junhao Jia, Yunyou Liu, Cheng Yang, et al.
- Uses hyperbolic GNNs for fMRI analysis
- Outperforms Euclidean baselines in psychiatric disorder classification
- Pioneers a new geometric paradigm for computational psychiatry

**Spatiotemporal Learning of Brain Dynamics from fMRI** (March 2025)
- Authors: Sangyoon Bae, Junbeom Kwon, Shinjae Yoo, Jiook Cha
- Introduces Multi-Band Brain Net (MBBN)
- First transformer-based framework for frequency-specific spatiotemporal brain dynamics

**EEG Foundation Challenge** (June 2025)
- Authors: Bruno Aristimunha, Dung Truong, et al.
- Cross-task and cross-subject EEG decoding
- Aims to contribute to computational psychiatry and neurotechnology breakthroughs

### 2.4 Clinical Applications

**A Narrative-Driven Computational Framework for Clinician Burnout Surveillance** (September 2025)
- Authors: Syed Ahmad Chan Bukhari, Fazel Keshtkar, Alyssa Meczkowska
- Analyzes ICU clinical narratives for burnout detection
- Specialty-specific findings: elevated burnout in Radiology, Psychiatry, and Neurology

**Metabolomic Biomarker Discovery for ADHD Diagnosis** (January 2026)
- Authors: Nabil Belacel, Mohamed Rachid Boulassel
- Integrates urinary metabolomics with interpretable ML
- Biology-based diagnostic frameworks for precision psychiatry

## 3. Emerging Trends

### 3.1 Multimodal Approaches
- Combining text, speech, neuroimaging, and physiological data
- Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) becoming dominant architecture
- Hybrid approaches combining multiple model types

### 3.2 Clinical High-Risk Population Research
- Focus on early detection and intervention
- Biomarker development for psychosis prediction
- Precision psychiatry approaches

### 3.3 Federated Learning
- Addressing privacy concerns in mental health data
- Handling subtype confounding in large-scale neuroimaging
- Cross-institutional collaboration without data sharing

## 4. Technical Challenges

1. **Interpretability vs. Performance Trade-off**
   - Traditional models (RL) offer interpretability but lack realism
   - LLMs generate realistic behaviors but lack structural interpretability
   - Hybrid frameworks emerging as solution

2. **Data Limitations**
   - Small sample sizes in clinical studies
   - Reproducibility issues
   - Disease subtype heterogeneity

3. **Clinical Validation**
   - Gap between research and clinical deployment
   - Regulatory considerations
   - Ethical concerns around AI in mental health
